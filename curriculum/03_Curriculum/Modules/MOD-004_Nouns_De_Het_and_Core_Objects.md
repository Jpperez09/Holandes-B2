## 4. Grammar

### 4.1. Pattern: `de-het-article`

**Pattern slug:** `de-het-article`
**Pienemann stage:** 1 (lexical assignment)

**Rule:** Every Dutch noun is either **de**-gender (common, ~70%) or **het**-gender (neuter, ~30%). Assignment is mostly unpredictable from the noun form alone and **must be learned with the noun** (per D-008).

**Predictability heuristics (not rules):**
- Diminutives in `-je` are always *het* (*het meisje*, *het brood**je***).
- Many monosyllabic concrete nouns are *de* (*de stoel, de tafel, de hand*).
- Languages and metals are typically *het* (*het Nederlands, het goud*).
- Verb-derived nouns (the infinitive used as a noun) are *het* (*het lezen, het werken*).

> Real rule: **memorize the article every time. Heuristics fail too often to rely on.**

### 4.2. Pattern: `present-tense-zijn-hebben` — *hebben*

| Subject | Form |
|---|---|
| ik | **heb** |
| jij / je | **hebt** *(also `heb jij` in inversion!)* |
| u | **hebt** / **heeft** *(both accepted)* |
| hij / zij / het | **heeft** |
| wij / we | **hebben** |
| jullie | **hebben** |
| zij (plural) | **hebben** |

**Examples:**
- *Ik heb een broer.*
- *Hij heeft een hond.*
- *Wij hebben een huis in Amsterdam.*

**Trap:** Inversion → `Heb jij…?` drops the `-t` on `hebt`. This is one of the few Dutch verbs where inversion changes the form.

### 4.3. Pattern: `plural-formation`

**Pattern slug:** `plural-formation`

**Two main rules:**

| Suffix | When | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| `-en` | Most native Dutch nouns | *huis → huizen, kamer → kamers... *wait, no — see below* |
| `-s` | Loanwords; nouns ending in unstressed `-el, -en, -er, -je`; vowel-ending nouns | *kamer → kamers, tafel → tafels, koffie → koffies, meisje → meisjes* |

> Spelling change: short vowels followed by single consonant **double the consonant** in plural (`bed → bedden`, `bus → bussen`).
> Long vowels followed by single consonant **drop one vowel letter** in plural (`huis → huizen`, `boek → boeken`, `s/z and f/v alternate`).

This module introduces the basic pattern. Edge cases drilled across Levels 5–10.

### 4.4. Pattern: `noun-phrase-een`

**Pattern slug:** `noun-phrase-een`
**Form:** `een + [noun]` (indefinite, singular).

**Note:** *Een* (indefinite, /ən/) ≠ *één* (the number "one", /eːn/). Same spelling without the accent; pronunciation differs.

## 5. Pronunciation

| Focus | Why |
|---|---|
| Consonant clusters | `huis` ends in `/-ys/`; `koffie` has `/kɔ/`; cluster-readiness matters for fluency. |
| `au/ou` → `/ʌu/` | *koud, oud, mouw, blauw*. New diphthong added to the palette. |
| `de` vs `dé` | Schwa vs full vowel. *De huis* (incorrect article but illustrative) ≠ *Dé huis* (emphatic). |
| `eu` → `/øː/` | *keuken, deur, kleur*. Front-rounded vowel; no Spanish equivalent. |
