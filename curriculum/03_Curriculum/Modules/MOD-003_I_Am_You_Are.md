## 4. Grammar

### 4.1. Pattern: `present-tense-zijn-hebben` — *zijn*

**Pattern slug:** `present-tense-zijn-hebben`
**Pienemann stage:** 2 (canonical SVO with irregular verb)

**Rule:** *Zijn* is irregular and high-frequency. Memorize all six forms as a block.

| Subject | Form |
|---|---|
| ik | **ben** |
| jij / je | **bent** |
| u (formal) | **bent** |
| hij / zij / het | **is** |
| wij / we | **zijn** |
| jullie | **zijn** |
| zij (plural) | **zijn** |

**Examples:**

- *Ik ben Colombiaans.*
- *Jij bent leraar.*
- *Hij is jong.*
- *Wij zijn studenten.*

**Contrast with Spanish:** Spanish has two verbs (*ser* and *estar*); Dutch has one — *zijn* covers both. Decision relief.

**Contrast with English:** Same six-form pattern; *ik ben* ≈ *I am*. Easier than the irregular Spanish *ser* paradigm.

**Trap:** Inversion `Ben ik …?` keeps the form (`ben`) the same. Subject-verb inversion does not change the verb form.

### 4.2. Pattern: `ik-kom-uit`

**Pattern slug:** `ik-kom-uit`
**Form:** `[subject] + [komen-form] + uit + [country]`

| Form |
|---|
| *Ik kom uit Colombia.* |
| *Zij komt uit Spanje.* |
| *Wij komen uit Nederland.* |

**Note:** Drill the *t*-ending on `komt`, `werkt`, `spreekt` (3rd person sg).

### 4.3. Pattern: `ik-spreek`

**Pattern slug:** `ik-spreek`
**Form:** `[subject] + [spreken-form] + [language]`

| Form |
|---|
| *Ik spreek Spaans en Engels.* |
| *Hij spreekt Portugees.* |
| *Wij spreken Nederlands.* |

### 4.4. Pattern: `dit-is-dat-is`

**Pattern slug:** `dit-is-dat-is`
**Form:** `Dit/Dat + is + [noun phrase]`

| Form |
|---|
| *Dit is mijn vriend.* |
| *Dat is een Nederlander.* |

## 5. Pronunciation

| Focus | Why |
|---|---|
| Final devoicing | `Nederland → /ˈneːdərlɑnt/`; *land → lant*. Dutch devoices voiced obstruents at word-end. |
| Nationality stress | *Colombiáans*, *Nederlánds*, *Portuguées* — stress on the final long syllable. |
| Schwa carry-over | *Nederlandse*, *Spaanse* — final `-e` is schwa, not `/e/`. |
