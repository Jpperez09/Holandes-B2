## 4. Grammar

### 4.1. Pattern: `ik-heet`

**Pattern slug:** `ik-heet` (see [[../../05_Exercises/Generated/Grammar_Patterns_A0_A1]])
**Pienemann stage:** 1 (lexical chunk)

**Rule:** Dutch `heten` ("to be called") behaves like Spanish *llamarse* but is **not** reflexive. The pattern is `[subject] + [conjugated heten] + [name]`. Used as a fixed chunk in this module.

**Examples:**

| Dutch | English |
|---|---|
| Ik heet Juan. | I'm called Juan / My name is Juan. |
| Jij heet Juan. | You're called Juan. |
| Hij heet Pieter. | He's called Pieter. |

**Contrast with Spanish:** Dutch does **not** mark this verb as reflexive (no *me*/*te*). *Yo me llamo Juan* → *Ik heet Juan* (no *me*).

**Contrast with English:** Dutch has both *Ik heet Juan* (more common in real life) and *Mijn naam is Juan* (more formal). English defaults to the equivalent of the second.

## 5. Pronunciation

| Focus | Why this module |
|---|---|
| Word stress in greeting phrases | `goedemórgen`, `goedemíddag`, `tot zíens` — stress placement matters for intelligibility. |
| Schwa reduction | All `goede-` greetings have schwas in unstressed syllables. Resist the Spanish habit of pronouncing every `e` clearly. |
| Final `-n` deletion | In casual northern Dutch, `goedemorgen` often becomes `goedemorge`. Recognize this; do not panic. |
| Numbers | Drill `zeven`/`negen` — schwa-final `-en` is reduced. `acht` carries `/x/`. `vijf` carries `/ɛi/`. |

### Mini-drill

1. Repeat each greeting after the audio model 3× each.
2. Count from 0 to 10 once slowly (full articulation), once at conversational speed.
3. Find any word with `/x/`, `/ɛi/`, `/œy/`, or schwa in this lesson's vocabulary and read it aloud 2×.
